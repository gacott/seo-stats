Methodology

How each statistic is sourced, graded and revised — and the limits you should keep in mind when quoting one.

On this page
CollectionConfidence grading LimitsCorrectionsCiting

Collection

Entries are drawn from three kinds of source: published industry studies, crawl and field datasets, and consumer or practitioner surveys. For each entry we record the source, its publication month, the stated sample and a short method note.

We do not commission the studies we cite. Where a figure is produced by a vendor with a commercial interest in the result, the source field names that vendor, and the confidence grade reflects the methodological transparency they provide.

Confidence grading

Every entry carries one of three grades.

  • High — large sample or full-population field data, transparent method, and a result that has been reproduced by a second source.
  • Medium — adequate sample and a stated method, but limited reproduction or a known source of bias.
  • Low — small sample, self-reported figures, or a method that is not fully disclosed.

The grade is a property of the evidence for a figure, not of how useful the figure is. A low-confidence number can still be the best available estimate.

Limits

Most of these statistics describe populations — large sites, particular industries, or panels of practitioners. They are not predictions for any individual site. A share figure in particular is meaningless without its denominator.

Read before quoting. A statistic that describes the average of a panel does not describe your site. Use the reference to set expectations and to choose what to measure, not to forecast a specific outcome.

Corrections

When a figure is superseded, we do not silently replace it. The entry is updated, the change is recorded on the changelog, and the previous value is noted where it materially changes a conclusion.

If you believe an entry is wrong, write to research@seofacts.org with the entry id and the evidence. We aim to respond within five working days.

Citing
