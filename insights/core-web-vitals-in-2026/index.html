Core Web Vitals in 2026: a floor, not a finish line

By Ivo Petrov · 2026-01-06
Insights
All insightsStatisticsMethodology

Of top-ranking pages, 64.7% pass all three Core Web Vitals thresholds. That means roughly a third of pages that rank do not — a useful reminder that performance is a tie-breaker, not a gate.

The more interesting figure is the distribution. Passing in the lab is easy; passing at the 75th percentile of real users, on real devices, is not. Our data shows a persistent gap between synthetic and field results, and that gap is where most performance work actually pays off.

Where the wins are

Time to first byte sits at a median of 0.6 seconds across top pages, and fully-loaded time at 1.8 seconds. The remaining variance is usually images and third-party scripts, not the server.

Treat the thresholds as a floor. The sites that win consistently are the ones whose field data does not regress after a release.
