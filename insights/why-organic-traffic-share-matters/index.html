Why the organic traffic share is the number to watch

By Dr. Mara Ellison · 2026-01-15
Insights
All insightsStatisticsMethodology

Organic search remains the largest single source of website traffic in the 2026 reference, at 67.6%. That number is widely quoted, and it is also widely misused. A share is a ratio, and a ratio without its denominator tells you very little.

The figure comes from a weighted panel of 1,247 marketing leaders across 14 markets. Because it is self-reported and then reconciled against analytics exports, it describes the sites in the panel — reasonably large, reasonably mature properties — rather than the whole web. Small sites and local businesses often sit well below it.

What the share does tell you

As a planning anchor, the share is useful: it tells a team that organic is not a side channel to be funded after paid media. When the share drops sharply without a corresponding drop in rankings, the cause is usually a change in measurement rather than a change in search.

What it does not tell you

It does not tell you what share your site should expect, and it does not tell you that organic traffic is growing. A rising share can coexist with falling absolute traffic if paid media is cut. Read the share alongside position-level CTR and zero-click behaviour before drawing conclusions.

Treat every share as a claim about a population, and ask which population before you quote it.
